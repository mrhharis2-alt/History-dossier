Chamber Four · The Last Door

The Verdict Hall

There is no lock on this final door — only a ledger, waiting for your judgement. Weigh the evidence from both sides.

It was too harsh

  • More negotiation, rather than simply imposing terms, might have made it less harsh.
  • Because it was a diktat, Germany could later claim it was not morally binding — that it wasn't bound to keep its terms.
  • The bitterness and resentment Germans felt over the Treaty was used by Hitler and the Nazis in the 1920s–30s in their rise to power.

It was fair (or lenient)

  • The Treaty was lenient compared with the terms Germany itself had imposed on Russia at Brest-Litovsk in March 1918.
  • Germany had forced the Bolsheviks to hand over half of Russia's industrial land — a harsher diktat than the one Germany received.
  • At the time, there was strong conviction that Germany had caused great destruction and misery and must be punished — some felt the Treaty could have been harsher still.

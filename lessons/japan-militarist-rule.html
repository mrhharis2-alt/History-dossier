Room 一 · Government Files

Consolidating the Government

Follow the classified timeline, then judge what changed for ordinary Japanese people.

Factor

Increased Military Influence

The military steadily took over roles once held by civilian politicians, from the civil service to the Foreign Affairs Ministry.

Factor

Control Factions in the Japanese Government

Rival military factions fought for control; the failed February 26 coup let the pragmatic Control Faction seize the army.

Factor

Loss of Political Power for Civilian Politicians

From Konoe's Cabinet Planning Office to Tojo's premiership, civilian politicians lost control of government and finance.

1934

Military Enters the Civil Service

The military strengthened its influence within the government. Military officers could now hold posts usually reserved for civilian politicians, and the Foreign Affairs Ministry handed supervision of Manchuria over to the military.

1935

“Electoral Purification”

The military government launched a campaign supposedly removing corrupt practices — but really targeting individuals and groups who did not accept militarist rule, such as socialists and communists.

26 Feb 1936

The February 26 Incident

Young officers of the radical Imperial Way Faction (Kōdōha) attempted to assassinate key government leaders, hoping to force a “Shōwa Restoration” honouring the Emperor and improving ordinary lives. The coup failed; the Emperor condemned it. The plotters were arrested, tried in a secret military court, and executed.

1936

The Control Faction Takes Over

The coup's failure destroyed the Imperial Way Faction's influence. The more pragmatic Control Faction (Tōseiha) seized control of the army, restored discipline, and had an active-duty General appointed Minister of War — a post previously held only by civilians. The military now began modernising for war.

1937

Konoe and the Cabinet Planning Office

Prince Konoe Fumimaro became Prime Minister as the military invaded China. Konoe permitted a Cabinet Planning Office staffed mostly by military officers (Oct 1937) — handing the military control of Japan's finances.

Oct 1941

Tojo Takes Command

Army Minister General Tojo Hideki became Prime Minister, signalling the military's total control over the Japanese government.

Room 二 · Economic Files

Controlling the Economy

Economic change is a more complicated case — look for both development and control.

Factor

Japan's Industrialisation

  • Shift from consumer industries to heavy industry and chemical production.
  • Production of arms and construction of roads, railways and bridges.
  • Government spending rose to stimulate the economy.
  • New zaibatsu (Nissan, Nichitsu, Mori) grew closely tied to the military.
Factor

Control of the Industry

  • 1931 Principal Industries Control Law let zaibatsu form cartels.
  • Cartels set up in shipbuilding and textiles.
  • By 1936, electric power was under direct government control.
  • Five-Year Plans set production targets by 1937.
Factor

Control of the Trade Unions

  • 1920s: government encouraged labour unions for stability.
  • By 1936, thousands of workers were forced to withdraw from unions.
  • Workers were pushed to focus on military production and lost many rights.
Factor

Improving the Countryside

  • By 1932, many farmers were in heavy debt.
  • 1932 Campaign for Economic Revitalisation encouraged scientific farming.
  • Many farmers signed up — but conditions barely improved since the government focused on industrialisation.
Think carefully: economic development can create benefits while increasing government control. Don't assume every outcome is simply “good” or “bad.”

Room 三 · Social Files

Controlling the People

Now investigate how the government shaped what people could think, read and learn.

Factor

Control of Japanese Thought

Ideas that did not conform to government beliefs, or were seen as threats to Japanese national identity, were censored. The Publications Monitoring Department and the 1936 Information and Propaganda Committee scrutinised all media. Socialists were imprisoned or dismissed. The kempeitai (military police) enforced this control.

Factor

Control of Education

Education stressed national pride, Japanese tradition, Shinto beliefs, loyalty to the Emperor, and community over the individual. The 1937 Fundamentals of Our National Polity guided teachers. The curriculum instilled patriotism and ultranationalist, militarist ideals — with heavy physical exercise, military-style drilling and technical lessons to prepare youths for war.
